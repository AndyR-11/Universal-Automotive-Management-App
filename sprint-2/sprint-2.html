Step 3 — Similar Products

We researched three existing products that provide features similar to the Universal Automotive Management App. Looking at these products helped us understand how similar applications reached their first release and what challenges they experienced.

Product A — Small Product

AUTOsist

Company: Founded by Zorrane Abdeali

Why it is similar: AUTOsist tracks vehicle maintenance, service records, fuel, and expenses.

Development started: Abdeali began working on AUTOsist in 2014.

First public release: 2014 to early 2015, when the application was initially shared with friends and car enthusiasts.

Time to first release: The exact development time is unknown, but it appears to have been around one year.

First release included: A consumer-focused application for storing and sharing vehicle maintenance information.

Team size: One person at launch.

What worked: The founder addressed a real problem he personally experienced: not having one place to view all of his vehicle maintenance history.

What did not work: Business users eventually wanted to use AUTOsist for vehicle fleets, which the original version did not support. The product later shifted toward commercial fleet management.

Sources:
Founder Interview
AUTOsist Website

Product B — Large Product

CARFAX Car Care

Company: CARFAX

Why it is similar: CARFAX Car Care provides vehicle service history, maintenance reminders, recall information, and vehicle information.

Development started: Around 2011.

First public release: August 22, 2012 on the App Store.

Time to first release: The exact development time is unknown, but it appears to have been approximately one to one-and-a-half years.

First release included: Vehicle service-history tracking, automatic service alerts, safety-recall information, and access through iPhone, Android, and the web.

Team size: Unknown.

What worked: CARFAX partnered with thousands of existing service shops, allowing vehicle service history to automatically appear instead of requiring users to enter everything manually.

What did not work: No specific issue was identified in the sources used by the team.

Step 4 — Comparison and Initial Estimate

After comparing our Release 1 capabilities, previous experience, and similar products, we identified the areas most likely to affect our project estimate.

Hidden Work

Testing and Data

Testing and data are the two hidden-work areas that appear most often in our Release 1 capabilities.

Similar products also experienced challenges related to vehicle data. AUTOsist was created to organize maintenance information, while CARFAX benefited from partnerships with service shops that automatically provided vehicle history.

This supports our belief that properly collecting, storing, and managing vehicle data will be an important part of our project.

Comparable Timelines

Focus on the Main Features First

AUTOsist appears to have taken around one year to reach its first release with one developer.

CARFAX appears to have taken around one to one-and-a-half years, although the team size is unknown. The development timeline for Drivvo is also unknown.

From these products, we learned that our team should focus on getting the most important features correct instead of trying to include everything in the first release.

Lessons Learned

What We Will Apply

One practice we want to copy is keeping the first release narrow and only including the functionality that is necessary.

One problem we want to avoid is limiting the application to certain countries, languages, or currencies. Our goal is for the application to eventually be usable internationally.

Initial Project Estimate

10–15 Months

Based on our previous project experience and the comparable products we researched, our initial estimate is that the first release of the Universal Automotive Management App could take approximately 10 to 15 months.

Estimated Team Size: 4 team members
