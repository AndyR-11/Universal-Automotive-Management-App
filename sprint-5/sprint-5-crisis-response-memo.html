1. Situation and triage

Last night an external provider that our reminder delivery component depends on released an update. Since then the component has returned incorrect results to every pilot user. Our engineers confirmed the cause is the provider’s update, not our code. The provider has published a fix, but we must retest the component against our charter’s success criteria before it returns to users. We selected reminder delivery as the affected component because it is our most data-dependent component (vehicle data and mileage, plus the third-party push notification and VIN services in our budget), its job is a charter success criterion, and it is the only component with published SLOs. We did not select the AI assistant because no SLO measures it.

Immediate consequences for the project

  • Reliability: Our published SLO is 99.9% of reminders correct and within 5 minutes each sprint, with an error budget of 5 misses per sprint. With every pilot reminder wrong since last night, that budget is spent.
  • Money: Our contingency reserve was $3,850. The earlier scope change (we assume is our added fuel-logging feature) used half (about $1,925) and this repair plus the provider’s emergency fees use another third (about $1,283), leaving about $642. Provider credits arrive 30 days after the incident closes.
  • Stakeholders: Our highest-interest stakeholder, vehicle owners in the pilot, spent a day acting on wrong output and nobody on our team told them.
  • People: Waldo, who wrote most of the reminder component and documented little, leaves in 10 business days. A hiring freeze means we can’t replace him and he is also the person who would normally apply the fix and retest.
  • Unverified: A public developer-forum post claims the update exposed customer data. The provider says it has no evidence and is still investigating. We do not know whether data was exposed.

3. Governance response

3.1 Incident owner

  • Who: Andres Romero, project manager and incident commander.
  • Authority: Under the charter he coordinates the response, makes decisions that do not materially change scope, and escalates scope, budget and schedule changes to you as sponsor.
  • Why him: He already holds the incident commander role on our escalation path and owns the schedule and budget, so decisions about people and money do not need a second hand-off.

3.2 Risk classification

We classify the reminder component using the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. We chose it over the EU AI Act tiers because the Act’s tiers turn on use-case categories (our app would likely be minimal risk) and would hide the real concern: a missed or wrong reminder can lead a user to skip a brake or tire service, a safety and cost consequence for a user, and uneven errors can harm one group more than another.

  • Map: Document what the component does, what data it uses from the provider, who is affected, and who is harmed first.
  • Measure: Track correctness by group, not only overall.
  • Manage: Retest after every provider change. Keep a rollback and a pause switch for reminders.
  • Govern: Name an incident owner, a bias-monitoring owner and a review cadence (3.1 and 3.3).

Added time: about 30 hours (risk and transparency documents (14 hrs), group-level metric and alert (12 hrs), review (4 hrs), roughly 9% of one sprint’s 320 team hours. It fits inside the next sprint if scope is held or reduced, and it does not move the end date on its own.

3.3 Bias monitoring plan

  • Metric: the share of reminders delivered correctly and within 5 minutes, calculated separately for users with older or less common vehicles and for everyone else, reported as the group’s miss rate divided by the overall miss rate.
  • Threshold: escalate to the incident owner within 24 hours if any group’s miss rate is more than twice the overall rate, or if any group falls below 99% correct in a week.
  • Owner: Carlos Adrian Yanez Duron (QA), with Andres Romero as backup.
  • Frequency: daily for the first two weeks after reminders are restored, then at the weekly Friday review.

This is an assumption-based first version; the group is an assumption because our records cannot yet tell us who was hit hardest.

4. Delivery and reliability impact

  • SLO breach: Reminder delivery (99.9% correct and within 5 minutes per sprint) was violated. The scenario says every pilot user received incorrect results, so the correct share fell from the 99.9% objective to approximately 0% for the period since last night’s update.
  • Error budget: Our budget is 0.1% of reminders, 5 misses in a sprint at about 5,000 reminders. Wrong results for every user spent it within the first few reminders. Our policy requires new feature work to pause, the fixers review the reminder log, find the cause and add a test or check before feature work resumes. The sponsor must be told in the next weekly update and users by the end of the week.
  • Dashboard metrics that changed: Share of reminders correct and within 5 minutes went down. Error budget remaining went to zero. We do not know whether latency changed, because our Sprint 4 service-level page measures correctness and lateness together in one share. We have no dashboard page with values so this is an assumption
  • Early detection: The reminder log is reviewed weekly on Fridays, so an overnight failure could not be caught. We would have needed an automated daily correctness check using test vehicles with known due dates, an alert when the correct share drops below the SLO, and a subscription to the provider’s change notices.

6. Personnel transition plan

Assumption: Waldo owns the reminder component, which sits in our Backend/API role. Our portal has no team topology diagram and no skills gap analysis. A topology diagram would have shown which team owns the component and how it interacts with others, and a skills gap analysis would have shown whether anyone besides Waldo can maintain it, so we use our staffing table instead.

Knowledge transfer

What we must capture before he leaves (assumed, because the component is mostly undocumented). How reminder due dates are calculated from vehicle data and mileage, how the provider’s output is read and what happens when it changes, configuration, deployment and rollback step, provider accounts and contacts, known quirks and test data.

Reassignment, cognitive load and skills gap

His work moves to the Backend/API role (Randal Guzman), with the PM / Backend role (Andres Romero) as second engineer and QA (Carlos Adrian Yanez Duron) reviewing. This raises the Backend/API role’s cognitive load, because it already carries the API work and now takes on a component it did not write. We cannot name the skills gap from a document that does not exist. Our assumption is that it is reminder scheduling and provider integration, which only Waldo knows.

Scope adjustment

If the receiving team cannot absorb the work, a candidate to remove is fuel logging (added in Sprint 3) or the AI assistant, because these features aren’t part of the system’s key workflow. Under the charter, a change this large needs team agreement and your approval.

7. The CEO briefing

Part A: Five key messages, in the order delivered

  1. An external provider update caused incorrect results for all pilot users, and the highest-interest stakeholder discovered the issue before your team notified them.
  2. The affected reminders should remain paused until the provider’s fix passes testing against the project charter.
  3. One user group received disproportionately incorrect results. Users with older or less common vehicles we’re affected more as maintenance information varies by make, model and year our vehicle databases are limited.
  4. Only about $642 of the original contingency reserve remains, and Waldo is leaving in ten business days.
  5. We believe the best way to move forward in addressing this crisis is to reduce the scope of the project. We need your approval.

Part B: What we will not say, and why

  • We will not claim customer data was exposed as the allegation is unverified, and the provider’s investigation is ongoing.
  • We will not say the provider’s fix completely solved the problem as we have not finished testing it against the charter.

Part C: Executive recommendation

  • Our recommendation is to cut the scope of the project. We will cut the fuel-logging feature added in sprint 3
  • Why: main features will still be protected, our contingency reserve is about to run out, and we need to reduce the workload of our team so they can learn Waldo’s component.
  • Cost: We won’t ask for more money, but we will use the remainder of the contingency reserve for retesting. We also need an additional 30 hours for risk classification. We estimate we will maintain our original deadline.
  • We need you to approve the scope change so we can continue with assessing the crisis.
  • If our team doesn’t need as much time to learn Waldo’s component, we might change to just continuing the project.
